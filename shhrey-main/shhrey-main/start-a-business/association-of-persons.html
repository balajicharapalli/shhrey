Association of Persons

A hybrid structure combining partnership flexibility with defined legal recognition — ideal for groups pursuing a common income objective.

What is an AOP?

An Association of Persons (AOP) is a group of individuals or entities that come together to earn income jointly. Under Indian income tax law, an AOP is treated as a separate taxable entity.

It is commonly used by professionals, co-investors, and joint venture partners who want a recognised structure without formal company incorporation. The AOP structure is flexible, relatively easy to form, and particularly useful for short-term or project-based collaborations.

Key Features
  • Recognised as a separate taxable unit under the Income Tax Act
  • Members enjoy defined liability based on their agreement
  • Flexible internal management and profit-sharing structure
  • No mandatory registration with MCA — simpler to form
  • Suitable for professionals, co-investors, and joint ventures
  • Income taxed at applicable slab or flat rates depending on structure

Important Considerations
  • AOP agreement must clearly specify profit-sharing ratios to avoid tax disputes
  • Annual income tax return filing is mandatory
  • If shares of members are indeterminate, maximum marginal rate of tax applies
  • Separate books of accounts must be maintained for the AOP
  • GST registration may be required based on turnover

Key Compliance Reminders

  • File ITR annually in the name of the AOP entity — not individual members
  • Maintain clear, separate books of accounts from day one
  • Assess GST registration requirement based on projected annual turnover
  • Review profit-sharing ratios with a CA before finalising the AOP deed

Trusts and Societies

Legal structures for non-profit organizations, charitable activities, and social welfare initiatives.

Overview

Trusts and Societies are commonly used for NGOs, charitable institutions, religious bodies and community welfare organizations in India. They provide a recognised legal framework to pool resources and work towards social, educational, or religious objectives without a profit motive.

While both structures serve similar purposes, they differ in governance, registration authority, and operational flexibility — making the right choice important for your organisation's long-term goals.

Key Differences

📜 Trust

  • Governed by the Indian Trusts Act
  • State-level registration with Sub-Registrar
  • Minimum 2 trustees required
  • Simpler formation process
  • Ideal for private charitable giving
  • Trust Deed is the governing document

🤝 Society

  • Governed by the Societies Registration Act
  • State-level registration with Registrar
  • Minimum 7 members required
  • Democratic management structure
  • Good for clubs, associations & welfare bodies
  • MOA & Rules are governing documents
  • No minimum capital requirement for either structure
  • Periodic compliance and annual filings required

Best For

Best For: Educational institutions, NGOs, religious bodies, welfare organizations.

Setup Process

1

Name & Objectives

Choose a unique name and clearly define your charitable or social objectives.

2

Draft Trust Deed / MOA

Prepare the Trust Deed or Memorandum of Association with all governing rules.

3

Registration with Authority

Register with the relevant state authority — Sub-Registrar or Registrar of Societies.

4

Apply for PAN & TAN

Obtain PAN and TAN for the registered organisation for tax and financial purposes.

5

Apply for 12A / 80G

Register for income tax exemption and donor deduction benefits under the IT Act.

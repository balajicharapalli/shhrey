Section 8 Company

A government-licensed non-profit company structure for organisations working towards charitable, educational, social, or environmental objectives.

What is a Section 8 Company?

A Section 8 Company is a type of company incorporated under Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013, specifically formed for promoting charitable, social, scientific, artistic, educational, or environmental objectives. It is the corporate equivalent of a Trust or Society — but with stronger legal standing, greater credibility, and better governance.

The key distinguishing feature is that a Section 8 Company cannot distribute profits or dividends to its members. Any income or surplus must be reinvested solely towards furthering the company's stated charitable objectives.

It requires a licence from the Central Government (Ministry of Corporate Affairs) and is exempt from using "Limited" or "Private Limited" in its name.

Key Features

  • Licensed and regulated under the Companies Act, 2013 by MCA
  • No minimum capital requirement for incorporation
  • Limited liability protection for all members and directors
  • Separate legal entity with perpetual succession
  • Exempt from using "Limited" or "Private Limited" in the company name
  • Profits cannot be distributed — must be used for stated objectives only
  • Eligible for tax exemptions under Sections 12A and 80G of the Income Tax Act
  • Higher credibility with donors, CSR funders, and government bodies
  • Can receive foreign donations under FCRA (Foreign Contribution Regulation Act)

Section 8 Company vs Trust vs Society

🏛️ Section 8 Company

  • Governed by Companies Act
  • Central Govt. licence
  • Separate legal entity
  • Limited liability
  • Highest credibility
  • Mandatory audit
  • Best for CSR & FCRA

📜 Trust

  • Governed by Trust Act
  • State-level registration
  • Not a separate entity
  • Unlimited liability
  • Simple to form
  • Audit may be optional
  • Good for private charity

🤝 Society

  • Governed by Societies Act
  • State-level registration
  • Limited recognition
  • Members liable
  • Moderate credibility
  • Audit required
  • Good for clubs & associations

Tax Benefits & Exemptions

  • Section 12A Registration — Exempts the company's income from income tax, provided funds are used for charitable purposes
  • Section 80G Registration — Allows donors to claim tax deduction on their donations, making fundraising significantly easier
  • Section 10(23C) — Additional exemptions available for educational and medical institutions
  • GST Exemptions — Certain charitable services are exempt from GST
  • FCRA Registration — Enables receipt of foreign contributions from overseas donors and organisations
  • CSR Funding — Companies with CSR obligations prefer to donate to Section 8 companies for compliance purposes

Best Suited For

NGOs, charitable foundations, educational institutions, research associations, sports bodies, environmental organisations, healthcare initiatives, and any entity working towards social welfare without profit motive.

Documents Required

For Each Director / Member:

  • PAN Card (mandatory)
  • Aadhaar Card / Passport / Voter ID (identity proof)
  • Latest bank statement or utility bill — not older than 2 months (address proof)
  • Passport-size photograph
  • Email ID and mobile number

For the Organisation:

  • Draft Memorandum of Association (MOA) with clearly stated charitable objectives
  • Draft Articles of Association (AOA)
  • Declaration by each director in Form INC-14 and INC-15
  • Estimated income and expenditure for next 3 years
  • Proof of registered office address (utility bill + NOC from owner)

Section 8 Company Registration Process

1

Obtain DSC & DIN

Digital Signature Certificate and Director Identification Number for each proposed director.

2

Name Approval

File RUN form on MCA portal to reserve the company name — no "Ltd." or "Pvt. Ltd." suffix needed.

3

Apply for Licence

File Form INC-12 with MCA to obtain the Central Government licence for non-profit status.

4

Receive Licence (INC-16)

MCA grants the licence via Form INC-16 after reviewing objectives and declarations.

5

File SPICe+ & Incorporate

Submit incorporation form with MOA, AOA, and licence to receive Certificate of Incorporation.

6

12A, 80G & FCRA

Apply for income tax exemptions and FCRA registration to activate fundraising capabilities.

Important Considerations

  • Profits and surplus cannot be distributed to members or directors under any circumstances
  • Conversion to any profit-making company is not permitted
  • Annual statutory audit is mandatory regardless of income
  • Detailed annual reports and financial statements must be filed with MCA every year
  • Any change in objectives requires Central Government approval
  • FCRA registration requires a minimum 3 years of domestic operation before applying
  • Directors must not draw remuneration unless specifically permitted by the licence

Ongoing Compliance

  • Annual Return (MGT-7) — Filed with ROC within 60 days of AGM
  • Financial Statements (AOC-4) — Filed annually with MCA
  • Statutory Audit — Mandatory every financial year by a qualified CA
  • Income Tax Return — Annual ITR filing mandatory even with tax exemptions
  • 12A & 80G Renewal — Periodic renewal required under new income tax rules
  • FCRA Annual Return — Mandatory if FCRA registered; filed with Ministry of Home Affairs
  • Board Meetings — Minimum 2 board meetings per year

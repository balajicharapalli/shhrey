Limited Liability Partnership

A hybrid structure combining partnership flexibility with the protection of limited liability — ideal for professional firms, startups, and collaborative businesses.

What is an LLP?

A Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) is a legally recognised business structure introduced under the LLP Act, 2008. It combines the operational flexibility of a traditional partnership with the limited liability protection of a private limited company.

In an LLP, each partner's liability is limited to their agreed contribution — meaning personal assets are protected from business debts. The LLP is a separate legal entity capable of owning property, entering contracts, and suing or being sued in its own name.

It is one of the most popular structures for CA firms, law firms, consulting agencies, and technology startups in India.

Key Features of an LLP
  • Minimum 2 designated partners — no upper limit
  • Limited liability — personal assets fully protected
  • Separate legal entity distinct from its partners
  • No minimum capital requirement to incorporate
  • Perpetual succession — LLP continues despite partner changes
  • Flexible structure governed by the LLP Agreement
  • Lower compliance burden than a Private Limited Company
  • Pass-through taxation — profits taxed at partner level
  • Easy to add or remove partners compared to companies
LLP vs Other Business Structures
🤝

LLP

  • Limited liability
  • Separate legal entity
  • Min. 2 partners
  • No minimum capital
  • Moderate compliance
  • Pass-through taxation
👥

Partnership Firm

  • Unlimited liability
  • Not a separate entity
  • Min. 2 partners
  • No minimum capital
  • Low compliance
  • Pass-through taxation
🏢

Private Limited Co.

  • Limited liability
  • Separate legal entity
  • Min. 2 directors
  • No minimum capital
  • High compliance
  • Corporate tax rate

Best Suited For

Professional service firms (CA, CS, law, consulting), technology startups, small and medium businesses with multiple partners, and any venture where partners want limited liability without the complexity of a private limited company.

Documents Required
For Each Partner
  • PAN Card (mandatory)
  • Aadhaar / Voter ID / Passport (identity proof)
  • Latest bank statement or utility bill (address proof)
  • Passport-size photograph
  • Email ID and mobile number
For Registered Office
  • Utility bill (electricity / water) — not older than 2 months
  • NOC from property owner (if rented)
  • Rental agreement (if applicable)
LLP Registration Process
1

Obtain DIN & DSC

Apply for Director Identification Number and Digital Signature Certificate for each designated partner.

2

Name Approval

File RUN-LLP form on the MCA portal to reserve your preferred LLP name.

3

Incorporation Filing

Submit FiLLiP (Form for Incorporation of LLP) with all required documents on MCA.

4

Certificate of Incorporation

Receive Certificate of Incorporation with LLPIN from the Registrar of Companies.

5

Draft LLP Agreement

File Form 3 (LLP Agreement) within 30 days of incorporation on MCA.

6

PAN, TAN & Bank

Apply for PAN, TAN, and open a current bank account in the LLP's name.

Advantages of an LLP
  • Partners' personal assets fully protected from business liabilities
  • Easy to form and maintain with fewer regulatory filings
  • No mandatory audit if turnover below ₹40 lakhs or contribution below ₹25 lakhs
  • Flexible profit-sharing through the LLP Agreement
  • Recognised structure for government tenders and contracts
  • Can convert to a Private Limited Company in the future
  • Foreign nationals can be designated partners (with RBI approval)

Ongoing Compliance
  • Form 11 — Annual Return filed within 60 days of financial year end
  • Form 8 — Statement of Accounts & Solvency filed within 30 days of 6 months of FY end
  • Income Tax Return — Annual ITR filing mandatory regardless of profit or loss
  • GST Returns — Monthly / quarterly returns if registered under GST
  • Audit — Mandatory if turnover exceeds ₹40 lakhs or contribution exceeds ₹25 lakhs
